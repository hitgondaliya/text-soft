DEVANAGARI: HOW SHREE'S 7 COMPOSER (S8Dev764.dll) WRITES UNICODE TEXT IN SHREE-LIPI 7 BYTES
=========================================================================================
Ground truth: dev7-groundtruth.json, built from what S8DevU64.dll (TutorGetMappedCodeW, Unicode) and
S8Dev764.dll (TutorGetMappedCode, raw bytes) answer for the same key:
  - every key of the 39 shipped .DEV keyboards (layer 0, Ctrl+Space, Ctrl+Shift+1): 6262 rows
  - 31035 probe sequences in Shree's typing order (i-sign struck first, reph struck after base+sign),
    47 per probe keyboard file, 661 files (copies of ITR.DEV with the 48 conjunct slots rewritten)
  -> 37239 pairs, 28546 Unicode strings, 27175 with an accepted answer (truth), 1305 quirk rows.
Bytes are hex, one per glyph code. "C" below = any consonant. Each rule is followed by examples
straight from the truth table (Unicode=bytes). The computed tables behind every rule are appended
at the end (APPENDIX).

Which fonts this is true for
----------------------------
- SHREE-DEV7-0700 / -4914 (S0700892.TTF, S4914892.TTF) and Shree-Dev7-001/002 (WDEV7001/2): yes - every byte
  the composer writes has a glyph there except 3C (see "cannot draw").
- SHREE-DEV-0700 / -4914 (4/5/6, S<dddd>0F2.TTF): yes as well. Of the four family-differing codes
  (91 92 B3 B6) the composer never writes 91, 92 or B6, and writes B3 only for the quote ” (a quirk in
  both families). It also never uses the 4/5/6-only ligatures at U+0200-U+0218.
- SHREE-SAN7-0960/0962/0964: NO. The Sanskrit composers S8SanU64/S8San764 give the same Unicode for the
  same keys but different bytes for 30903 of 30939 probes (क = 40 A1 there, 48 24 here; ध = 52 22 vs
  59). Rendered check: DEV7 bytes in a SAN7 font draw garbage, the Sanskrit composer's bytes draw the
  right text. Sanskrit typed in a DEV7 font uses this file; SAN7 fonts need san7-groundtruth.json
  (same capture, same format, built from the Sanskrit composers; its rules were not analysed here).
  NOTE: the TextSoft config lists the SHREE-SAN7 fonts with enc=shree-dev456 - that is wrong.

1. Letters, spacers, half forms
-------------------------------
- Most letters are one byte: ख=49 ग=4A घ=4B च=4D ज=4F झ=50 ण=55 त=56 थ=57 द=58 ध=59 न=5A प=6E ब=7E
  भ=5E म=5F य=60 र=61 ल=62 व=64 श=65 ष=66 स=67 ह=68 ळ=69.
- Two letters carry the spacer 24 after them: क=48 24, फ=5C 24 (also ऊ=44 24, ऋ=46 24, ॠ=47 24,
  रू=EA 24, क्क=B8 24, क्व=B9 24, क्त=BA 24). Six carry the spacer 3E: ङ=4C 3E छ=4E 3E ट=51 3E
  ठ=52 3E ड=53 3E ढ=54 3E (and their ligatures ट्ट=C5 3E ठ्ठ=C7 3E ड्ड=C8 3E ढ्ढ=C9 3E ष्ट=EF 3E).
  ञ=C4 6D and त्त=CE 6D use the aa-bar 6D as their stem.
- Half forms are one byte: क्=8A ख्=BB ग्=BD घ्=BF च्=C0 ज्=C1 झ्=C2 ञ्=C4 ण्=CA त्=CB थ्=CF ध्=DC
  न्=DD प्=DF फ्=E2 ब्=E3 भ्=E4 म्=E5 य्=E6 ल्=EB व्=EC श्=ED ष्=EE स्=F1 ह्=F4 ळ्=F9, क्ष्=FA
  श्र्=FB त्र्=CD त्त्=CE ख़्=99.
- Letters with no half form get the halant B2 instead, placed before their spacer:
  ङ्=4C B2 3E छ्=4E B2 3E ट्=51 B2 3E ठ्=52 B2 3E ड्=53 B2 3E ढ्=54 B2 3E द्=58 B2.
- A sequence ending in a Link (code 64) is answered with the half form the composer is waiting with
  (क् = 8A, kind canonical:pending-link). A visible final virama is the halant key (code 44, Unicode
  ्+ZWNJ): क्‌ = 48 B2 24, ख्‌ = 49 B2. The explicit half (code D7, ्+ZWJ) = the half form: क्‍ = 8A.

2. Vowel signs and the spacer order
-----------------------------------
Signs: ा 6D, ी 72, ु 77, ू 79, ृ A5, ॄ A6, े 6F, ै A1, ो 6D 6F, ौ 6D A1, ॅ B0, ॉ 6D B0, ं A7,
ँ B1, ः 85. Sign ligatures: ि+ं 71, ी+ं 74, े+ं 7C, ै+ं A2, ो+ं 6D 7C.
- After a 24-letter (क फ ...): signs below (ु ू ृ ॄ) and above (े ै ं ँ ॅ, े+ं, ै+ं) go BEFORE the 24;
  ा ी ो ौ ॉ ः go after it. After a 24 the ी is 73 (not 72) and ीं is 73 A8 (not 74).
  कु=48 77 24  के=48 6F 24  कं=48 A7 24  कैं=48 A2 24  का=48 24 6D  की=48 24 73  कीं=48 24 73 A8
  कः=48 24 85  को=48 24 6D 6F
- After a 3E-letter (ङ छ ट ठ ड ढ): only the signs below go before the 3E; everything else after.
  टु=51 77 3E  टृ=51 A5 3E  टे=51 3E 6F  टं=51 3E A7  टी=51 3E 72  टीं=51 3E 74
- Other letters: letter, then sign. खु=49 77  खी=49 72  खीं=49 74  खें=49 7C
- u/uu forms: after द and ह (and ligatures ending in द) ु=FE ू=FF: दु=58 FE हू=68 FF द्धु=D5 FE
  न्दु=DD 58 FE; under stacked ligatures ु=78: ङ्गु=93 78 ट्टु=C5 78 3E; र has its own: रु=E9
  रू=EA 24. ृ ligatures: दृ=D1 हृ=F6 (others: कृ=48 A5 24).

4. The ra-sign (code D1, Unicode C्र)
-------------------------------------
- AB after the letter, before its 24: क्र=48 AB 24  फ्र=5C AB 24  ग्र=4A AB  ब्र=7E AB  ञ्र=C4 6D AB
- B4 under the round-bottomed ङ छ ट ठ ड ढ (before the 3E) and ळ: ट्र=51 B4 3E  ड्र=53 B4 3E
  ङ्र=4C B4 3E  ळ्र=69 B4
- one-glyph ligatures: ख्र=BC  झ्र=C3  त्र=CC  द्र=D0  प्र=E0  श्र=6C  स्र=F2  ह्र=F5
  (श्र and त्र also have codes of their own: 45 -> 6C, 47 -> CC; code EA -> FB 6D; श typed with the
  ra-sign gives 65 AB - all accepted, truth prefers the ligature code)
- with ु/ू: AB 77 / AB 79 (क्रु=48 AB 77 24, क्रू=48 AB 79 24), ligature + 77/79 (प्रु=E0 77,
  त्रू=CC 79), and AE / AF for the B4 group (ट्रु=51 AE 3E, ड्रू=53 AF 3E).
- र + ra-sign gives 61 AB, but Unicode र्र is a reph on र: truth र्र = 61 A9, 61 AB kept as alt.
- C + Link + र gives half C + full र (क्र = 8A 61, क्रु = 8A E9): a visibly different form, kept as
  alt:C+link+ra; truth क्र = 48 AB 24.
- ई is इ + the reph glyph: ई=42 A9 (typed इ + reph), ईं=42 AA.

5. The ya-sign (code D5) and य after a Link
-------------------------------------------
- D5 writes E7 after the letter and its spacer: क्य=48 24 E7  ट्य=51 3E E7  ख्य=49 E7.
- Through the Link: half + य (क्य=8A 60, ख्य=BB 60), E7 for letters with no half form (ट्य=51 3E E7),
  or a ligature (द्य=DA, ह्य=F8). Both orders are accepted (kinds ya-sign code D5 / link+ya); truth
  prefers a vendor key, then the shorter answer (क्य -> 8A 60, द्य -> DA).

6. The reph (code 40, struck AFTER the letter and its signs)
-----------------------------------------------------------
- A9 goes after the syllable's glyphs, but BEFORE a trailing 24 and AFTER a trailing 3E or 6D:
  र्क=48 A9 24  र्कु=48 77 A9 24  र्कृ=48 A5 A9 24  र्का=48 24 6D A9  र्की=48 24 73 A9
  र्ट=51 3E A9  र्टु=51 77 3E A9  र्प=6E A9  र्पु=6E 77 A9  र्स्त=F1 56 A9  र्ङ्ग=93 A9
- sign + reph ligatures: े+reph 7D (र्के=48 7D 24, र्टे=51 3E 7D), ै+reph A3, ें+reph 5D,
  ैं+reph A4, ं+reph AA (र्कं=48 AA 24), ी+reph 75 and ीं+reph 76 where the ी is not after a 24
  (र्पी=6E 75, र्पीं=6E 76; but र्की=48 24 73 A9, र्कीं=48 24 73 AA).
- reph struck before ं gives the AA ligature, ं struck before the reph gives A7 A9 (र्कं: 48 AA 24 /
  48 A7 A9 24); both accepted, truth prefers the shorter AA.
- other orders (kept as alt): reph struck first writes A9 before the letter (र्क=A9 48 24); reph struck
  before the vowel sign writes it before the sign (र्का=48 A9 24 6D); र + Link before a letter gives
  the eyelash ra E8 (र्क=E8 48 24; र्‍=E8 on its own is canonical, Unicode र्+ZWJ).

8. Nukta
--------
- Only letters with a code of their own get a nukta, written BEFORE the letter (B5, or 8B for the
  round-bottomed ड ढ ळ), or as a ligature: क़=B5 48 24 ख़=98 ग़=B5 4A ज़=B5 4F ड़=8B 53 3E
  ढ़=8B 54 3E फ़=B5 5C 24 य़=B5 60 ऩ=B5 5A ऱ=B5 61 ऴ=8B 69; क़ि=7B B5 48 24 ज़्य=B5 4F E7
  ख़्=99 ज़्=B5 C1. The Unicode composer writes these precomposed (U+0958-U+095F, U+0929, U+0931,
  U+0934), so those are the truth keys.
- The stand-alone nukta key (code EE, Ctrl+Space 3 on most keyboards): the Unicode composer writes
  U+093C (C + EE comes back decomposed, C + U+093C), but the 7 composer writes 9D - a short piece of
  headline with an advance, NOT a nukta dot - and 8F (the ÷ glyph) when a Link follows. Nukta code D9
  is dropped entirely. All of these are quirk:*; a decomposed C + U+093C has NO truth: normalise to the
  precomposed letter (NFC does not do that - U+0958-095F are composition exclusions).

10. What DEV7 cannot draw (quirk:*, no truth)
---------------------------------------------
- ॢ ॣ ॡ, ॑ ᳚ ॰ ॺ, ॥, “ : the 7 composer writes nothing for them (a sign is dropped, the rest kept).
  "। ।" comes back 26 26 while the Unicode composer joins it to ॥.
- ऌ -> 2A (the * glyph).  ॆ -> 90, an empty glyph (ऎ=45 90, ऒ=41 6D 90, ऄ=41 90, कॆ=48 24 90 all
  lose the mark).  ₹ -> 3C: no glyph at 3C in any installed DEV7 or DEV 4/5/6 font.
- ” -> B3: B3 draws क् in a 7 font and a zero-width ्न in a 4/5/6 font - neither is a quote.
- a Link after the ra-sign (क्र् typed C, ra-sign, Link) is dropped: 48 AB 24, same as क्र.

11. The family-differing codes (91 92 B3 B6)
--------------------------------------------
Measured over all 37239 pairs: 91, 92 and B6 never occur; B3 occurs once, for ” (quirk above).
The ligatures 4/5/6 keeps at those codes come back built from pieces: ङ्क=4C B2 3E 48 24
ङ्ख=4C B2 3E 49 ङ्म=4C B2 3E 5F. The codes next to them that look like 4/5/6 ligatures are the same
glyph in both families and are used: 93 ङ्ग, 94 ङ्घ, 95 ह्न, 96 ड्ढ, AC ङ्क्ष.
The 4/5/6-only ligatures at U+0200-0218 are never produced; the 7 composer writes instead:
क्क=B8 24 क्ल=8A 62 क्व=B9 24 ङ्क=4C B2 3E 48 24 ङ्ख=4C B2 3E 49 ङ्ग=93 ङ्घ=94 ङ्म=4C B2 3E 5F
ङ्क्ष=AC छ्व=4E 89 3E ञ्च=23 6D ञ्ज=C4 4F प्ल=DF 62 फ्ल=E2 62 ल्ल=84 ट्व=51 89 3E ट्ट=C5 3E
ट्ठ=C6 3E ठ्ठ=C7 3E ड्ड=C8 3E ड्ढ=96 द्ढ=58 B2 54 3E क्त=BA 24 न्न=DE प्त=E1
(ल has two glyphs: 62 from code 6D, most keyboards, and the one after a half letter; 63 from codes DF/E7.)

12. Where the Unicode composer is wrong (quirk:U-composer-wrote-*, 199 rows, not used)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- C + ya-sign + reph -> "Cर्" (42+8 rows; 7: क्य + A9)      - C + ः + reph -> "Cर्ः" (42+8)
- i + C + ya-sign -> "Cि्य" (40+8; 7: 7B 48 24 E7)          - i + C + nukta -> "Cि़" (34)
- अ + sign + candrabindu -> "अअँ"; इ + ं + reph -> "इई"; doubled ं/ँ collapsed; "। ।" -> "॥".

13. Typing orders kept as alt:* (not truth)
-------------------------------------------
reph struck first (र्क=A9 48 24), reph before the vowel sign (र्का=48 A9 24 6D), ra + Link + C
(eyelash: र्क=E8 48 24), C + Link + र (क्र=8A 61), र + ra/ya-sign (र्र=61 AB, र्य=61 E7).

Truth selection (same as Gujarati): per Unicode string, answers from vendor keys and canonical-order
probes are accepted; preferred = an answer typed with a ligature code, then the one most vendor keys
give, then a canonical one, then the shortest.
